# Terrain Collision Audit - Marching Squares

## Summary

**CRITICAL BUG FOUND: Collision shape vertices are in chunk-local tile coordinates (0-31 range) but the chunk body is positioned at `worldPositionMeters` which is also in tile units. This means collision vertices are LOCAL offsets from the body origin -- which is correct for Forge2D. However, the marching squares vertices range from 0 to ~31 (tile units within the chunk), and the body is at `(chunkX * 32, chunkY * 32)` in world tile units. So collision shapes ARE in the right coordinate space relative to their body.**

**The real problems are different -- see below.**

---

## Coordinate System Analysis

### Pod Spawn
- **File:** `lib/entities/pod/pod.dart:59`
- Pod spawns at `Vector2(0, -2)` in Forge2D world coordinates (meters/tiles -- same thing here since pixelsPerMeter=10 is only used for camera zoom, not physics scale)
- Forge2D world coordinates ARE tile coordinates in this game (1 Forge2D unit = 1 tile)
- Pod is at world tile position (0, -2), meaning 2 tiles ABOVE y=0

### Forge2D / Camera Setup
- **File:** `lib/motherlode_game.dart:33`
- `zoom: GameConstants.pixelsPerMeter` = 10.0
- `Forge2DGame` uses zoom as the pixels-per-meter conversion
- In this game, 1 Forge2D "meter" = 1 tile in world coordinates
- Camera zoom of 10 means 10 screen pixels per Forge2D meter/tile

### Chunk Body Position
- **File:** `lib/world/chunk.dart:36-39`
- `worldPositionMeters = Vector2(chunkX * 32.0, chunkY * 32.0)` -- in tile units
- Body is created as `BodyType.static` at this position
- For chunk(0,0): body at (0, 0)
- For chunk(0,-1): body at (0, -32)

### Marching Squares Vertices
- **File:** `lib/world/marching_squares.dart:105-106`
- Vertices use `px = x.toDouble()` and `py = y.toDouble()` where x,y are LOCAL chunk indices (0 to 31)
- These are chunk-local coordinates, which is CORRECT because Forge2D adds them relative to the body position

### Collision Shape Construction
- **File:** `lib/world/chunk.dart:129-155`
- Vertices from marching squares (chunk-local, 0-31 range) are used directly as EdgeShape/ChainShape vertices
- EdgeShape and ChainShape vertices are LOCAL to the body -- this is correct Forge2D behavior
- The body is at `worldPositionMeters`, so a vertex at (5, 3) means world position (chunkX*32 + 5, chunkY*32 + 3)

## Surface Zone Analysis (`_handleSurfaceZone`)

**File:** `lib/world/world_generator.dart:511-545`

### What it does:
1. **worldY < 0** (above surface): ALL cells set to empty
2. **worldY == 0** (surface line):
   - Cells within |worldX| <= 3 are EMPTY (landing pad gap)
   - All other cells at worldY=0 are SOLID (sand, density 0.8)
3. **worldY > 0 with depth <= sandLayerEnd**: Existing solid cells become sand

### Does solid terrain exist at worldY=0?
**YES** -- but only for |worldX| > 3. The 7-tile-wide gap centered at x=0 is empty.

### Where is chunk(0,0)?
- Chunk(0,0) covers world tiles (0,0) to (31,31)
- Row y=0 of chunk(0,0) corresponds to worldY=0
- At worldX=0 through worldX=3, these cells are EMPTY (landing pad)
- At worldX=4 through worldX=31, these cells are SOLID sand

## Pod Fall-Through Scenario

### Pod trajectory:
1. Pod spawns at Forge2D position (0, -2) -- world tile (0, -2)
2. Gravity = 9.8 m/s^2 pulls pod DOWNWARD (positive Y in Forge2D = down)
3. Pod falls toward y=0 and below

### Terrain at the pod's path:
- At worldX=0, worldY=0: **EMPTY** (landing pad gap)
- At worldX=0, worldY=1 through worldY=31 (chunk 0,0): These are generated by noise + threshold, then `_handleSurfaceZone` only clears worldY<0 and sets worldY==0 landing pad. For worldY>0, cells keep their noise-generated values but get reclassified as sand.

### PROBLEM 1: Landing pad gap extends underground
The landing pad gap at |worldX| <= 3 only applies to worldY==0. But the noise-generated terrain below may also have caves/gaps right at x=0. The pod could fall through the single-tile-thick sand surface at y=0 gap into whatever is below.

**More importantly:** At worldX=0, worldY=0, the cell is EMPTY. The pod at x=0 will fall right through y=0 because there's no solid cell there. It will keep falling until it hits noise-generated terrain below.

### PROBLEM 2: The real collision gap
Looking at marching squares case 15 (all 4 corners solid): `edgeVerts = []` -- no collision edges are generated for fully solid 2x2 squares. This is CORRECT behavior since the boundary is only at solid/empty transitions.

However, for the surface at worldY=0:
- The 2x2 square at (x=3, y=0) in chunk(0,0) has corners:
  - TL (3,0) = EMPTY (landing pad), TR (4,0) = SOLID (sand)
  - BL (3,1) = depends on noise, BR (4,1) = depends on noise
- This creates a marching squares edge at the surface boundary

The collision IS generated at solid/empty boundaries. The pod at x=0 will simply fall through because the terrain IS empty there.

## CRITICAL FINDINGS

### Finding 1: No Ground Under Landing Pad
**Severity: HIGH**
The `_handleSurfaceZone` only makes worldY==0, |worldX|<=3 empty but does NOT ensure solid ground exists at worldY==1 (or any specific depth) beneath the landing pad. The pod spawns at (0, -2) and falls toward (0, 0+). Since worldY=0 at x=0 is empty, the pod continues falling into noise-generated terrain which may or may not be solid at that exact x position.

**Fix needed:** Either:
- Make worldY=1 (or some row just below y=0) guaranteed solid across all X values, OR
- Place a solid "landing pad floor" at worldY=1 for |worldX| <= 3

### Finding 2: Collision Shapes Are Correctly Positioned (Not a Bug)
The collision vertices ARE in chunk-local coordinates and the body IS positioned at `worldPositionMeters`. This is correct Forge2D usage. Vertices at (0-31, 0-31) are offsets from the body origin at (chunkX*32, chunkY*32).

### Finding 3: EdgeShape vs ChainShape Usage
- 2-vertex segments use EdgeShape -- correct
- 3+ vertex segments use ChainShape with `createChain` -- correct
- Friction 0.3, restitution 0.0 -- reasonable values

### Finding 4: Rendering vs Physics Coordinate Match
- TerrainRenderer translates canvas by `(chunkX * chunkSize, chunkY * chunkSize)` -- same as chunk body position
- Marching squares vertices are in the same 0-31 local space for both rendering and physics
- Visual and physics should align correctly

### Finding 5: Density Threshold Mismatch Possible
- `_interpolateEdge` uses threshold 0.52 for edge interpolation
- `_applyThreshold` uses `biome.solidThreshold` for solid/empty classification
- If `biome.solidThreshold` differs significantly from 0.52, edge interpolation points won't align with the actual solid/empty boundary, causing visual/collision mismatch
- **Minor issue** -- the collision edge would still exist, just slightly offset

### Finding 6: Step 8 Ordering Issue
`_handleSurfaceZone` runs as Step 8, AFTER cellular automata (Step 3). This means the CA passes could have already modified cells near the surface. The surface zone handler then overwrites them. This ordering is actually fine -- the surface handler has the final say.

However, `_handleSurfaceZone` does NOT run cellular automata again after its modifications, meaning the transition from the landing pad empty zone to solid terrain could be abrupt (no smoothing). This could create sharp collision edges but is not a fall-through bug.

## Recommended Fixes

1. **Guarantee solid ground under landing pad:** In `_handleSurfaceZone`, add a rule for worldY==1 (or 1-2) that forces cells to be solid sand at |worldX| <= 4, creating a landing pad floor the pod can rest on.

2. **Alternative: Move pod spawn onto solid ground.** Instead of spawning at y=-2 over an empty gap, spawn on top of known solid terrain. But this is harder since terrain is procedurally generated.

3. **Consider adding a dedicated landing pad body** -- a simple static rectangle at (0, 0) to (0, 1) width ~6 tiles, rather than relying on terrain generation to provide the floor.
